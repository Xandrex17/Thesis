2. SYNTHESIZED PARAGRAPH (Strict 5-Step Formula, Zero POV, Zero Proving/Supports)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Although multimodal sensor fusion integrating satellite Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) and coastal Automatic Identification System (AIS) telemetry represents the primary paradigm for identifying non-cooperative dark vessels, prevailing association architectures experience severe operational degradation in congested port environments. In a comprehensive review of maritime artificial intelligence, Hanmante et al. (2026) established that while combining radar and AIS reduces surveillance false alarms by 35% to 60% in open corridors, decision-level pairwise matching algorithms suffer an accuracy collapse below 75% in dense coastal waterways due to spatiotemporal latency and the absence of relational inter-vessel modeling. This operational vulnerability was documented empirically by Li et al. (2025), who achieved 91% vessel detection accuracy using oriented bounding boxes (YOLO-OBB) and global bipartite assignment (JVC), yet observed severe identity swapping, nearest-neighbor mismatching, and sensitivity to small-vessel clutter when targets cluster in close proximity. To address feature redundancy in multi-sensor pipelines, Xue et al. (2026) demonstrated that asymmetric multimodal fusion (AMFNet) guided by AIS spatial density priors enhances radar feature extraction over symmetric models, yet the approach remains vulnerable to performance drops during low-contrast radar anomalies or transponder dropouts. Crucially, a comparative synthesis of these investigations reveals that existing surveillance frameworks treat vessel association as an isolated, pairwise assignment problem, failing to account for dynamic navigational interdependencies among neighboring ships. To resolve this systemic bottleneck, the methodological architecture engineered by Zhang et al. (2023)—which achieved a 9.47% error reduction using gated spatiotemporal graph aggregation (G-STGAN) to model dynamic multi-ship maneuvering dependencies—provides the foundational framework for reformulating cross-modal AIS-SAR association as dynamic link prediction over relational vessel interaction graphs, thereby suppressing spurious dark-vessel false alarms in congested harbor anchorages.
