BATSTATEU CICS CS-4102 THESIS 1 & 2

A Cross-Modal Spatiotemporal Graph Framework for Detecting Dark-Vessel Candidates

Using AIS Trajectories and Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) Imagery • Batangas Bay Study Sector

01

Defensible Scientific Terminology

Cease presumption of guilt or declaring vessels definitively "illegal". Unmatched physical radar detections are framed probabilistically as "AIS-SAR Discrepancies" and "Dark-Vessel Candidates" requiring algorithmic corroboration and physical verification.

02

Clear Computer Science Novelty

Explicitly delineate third-party data collection from in-house algorithmic contributions. The novel CS core is the bipartite graph schema, GATv2 cross-modal attention matching model, and anchorage-aware context filter—not connecting external APIs.

03

Justification of Graph Modeling

Greedy nearest-neighbor matching collapses in Batangas Port because proximate ships cause identity swapping. The dynamic heterogeneous graph considers multi-target kinematics, spatial proximity, and neighborhood queues simultaneously before assigning correspondence.

04

Problem-to-Objective 1:1 Mapping

The 3 specific research problems map strictly to the thesis objectives: matching ambiguity (Obj 2.1), transmission gaps vs true dark events (Obj 1.2 & 3.3), and port false alarms (Obj 2.3 & 3.2), with a dedicated 4th objective for this interactive POC dashboard.

05

Quantitative Evaluation Metrics

Objective 3 formally establishes False-Positive Rate (FPR) as a primary benchmark alongside Precision, Recall, F1-Score, and Matching Accuracy, distinguishing cross-modal association accuracy from discrepancy detection performance.

06

Guardrailed Proof of Concept (POC)

Per BatStateU CS guidelines ("A thesis investigates; a project mainly builds"), the POC is an algorithmic demonstration and visualization testbed supporting the thesis, not an enterprise software product.

1. Dynamic Bipartite Graph Formulation • G = (V_AIS, V_SAR, E_candidate)

For each Sentinel-1 overpass epoch t_s, a dynamic heterogeneous graph is constructed in PyTorch Geometric (HeteroData):

  • AIS Node Feature Vector x_AIS ∈ ℝ^6: [lat, lon, SOG, COG, vessel_type_id, Δt_ping]
  • SAR Node Feature Vector x_SAR ∈ ℝ^7: [centroid_lat, centroid_lon, length_L, width_W, σ°_backscatter_dB, is_in_anchorage_bool, dist_to_shoreline]
  • Candidate Reachability Edge Vector e_ij ∈ ℝ^4: [Δd_euclidean, Δt_offset, cos(Δθ), Δv_reachability]
  • Local Neighborhood Context: Intra-modal neighbor edges within 2.0 km among AIS vessels and SAR detections for queue awareness.

2. Physical Basis for the 30-Minute Matching Window (±15 Min)

Commercial ships navigating Batangas fairways operate under regulated harbor speeds of 8 to 12 knots (4.1 to 6.2 m/s). Over a 15-minute window, maximum theoretical displacement is bounded between 3.7 and 5.5 km:

Max Displacement = 12 knots × 0.25 hours ≈ 3.0 NM ≈ 5.55 km (Reachability Gating Bubble)

Restricting association to ±15 min prevents cubic spline divergence while accommodating routine coastal VHF slot collision gaps (5 to 12 minutes; Chen et al., 2026; Li et al., 2025).

3. 128-Dimensional Shared Latent Embedding & GATv2 Attention

Modality-specific projection layers map disparate radar backscatter and AIS kinematics into a shared 128-dimensional latent space to eliminate dimensional bottlenecking. GATv2 layers dynamically compute link attention weights:

α_uv = softmax_v( LeakyReLU( a^T [ W_u h_u || W_v h_v || W_edge x_uv ] ) )

Trained using Focal Loss (γ = 2.0) to suppress easy negative edges (which constitute ~90% of candidate pairs in dense ports).

Class 2 Ambiguous / Low-Risk Target Unmatched targets < 25m in length (exempt under IMO SOLAS Chapter V Regulation 19), targets inside designated anchorage waiting grounds, or within 150m of shore/pier infrastructure. Strictly masked from loss calculation to prevent false-alarm penalization.
